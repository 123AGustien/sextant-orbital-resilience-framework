01 — RESEARCH CONTEXT

Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Research Framework

1.1 Background

Modern spacecraft and mission systems depend upon interconnected functions including communications, navigation, telemetry, power, guidance, software, ground infrastructure and human mission operations.

As system complexity increases, an abnormal condition in one subsystem may create secondary effects elsewhere. A communications degradation, for example, may affect telemetry availability, operator awareness, recovery coordination and overall mission resilience.

The Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Framework investigates whether a structured resilience layer can assist in recognising and managing such developing conditions within a controlled simulation environment.

1.2 Research Problem

The research problem is not simply the detection of an individual fault.

The principal concern is the behaviour of a system when several conditions interact over time.

The research therefore considers a sequence such as:

INITIAL ANOMALY

↓

SYSTEM STRESS

↓

SECONDARY EFFECT

↓

REDUCED SYSTEM MARGIN

↓

ADDITIONAL ANOMALY

↓

CASCADE RISK

↓

STABILIZATION / RECOVERY ASSESSMENT

The framework provides a controlled environment in which this behaviour can be simulated, recorded and examined.

1.3 Research Proposition

The central proposition is:

«A layered deterministic resilience architecture combining assessment, verification, controlled state transitions, recovery simulation, memory continuity, auditability and human authority may improve the recognition and structured management of developing abnormal conditions in a controlled orbital simulation environment.»

This proposition remains a hypothesis.

The framework therefore does not present successful simulations as proof of operational safety or superiority.

Instead, the proposition is intended to be:

- tested;
- challenged;
- measured;
- repeated;
- audited; and
- independently reviewed.

Negative or inconclusive results are valid research outcomes.

1.4 Research Objectives

The principal research objectives are to investigate whether the architecture can:

1. identify simulated abnormal conditions;
2. classify developing system severity;
3. maintain structured state transitions;
4. simulate possible recovery pathways;
5. provide an independent validation layer;
6. preserve historical simulation information;
7. maintain an auditable decision pathway;
8. support human decision-making;
9. provide repeatable scenario execution; and
10. provide a foundation for future high-fidelity validation.

1.5 Scope

The present research scope covers deterministic simulation and decision-support architecture.

Areas of investigation include:

- orbital-system resilience;
- abnormal-condition detection;
- scenario modelling;
- recovery-pathway simulation;
- failsafe state transitions;
- validation;
- memory continuity;
- auditability;
- cascade behaviour;
- human-in-the-loop decision-support; and
- governance.

The current implementation is not intended to constitute a certified spacecraft control system.

1.6 Research Boundaries

The framework deliberately separates research assessment from operational spacecraft control.

The following distinction is fundamental:

ASSESSMENT ≠ OPERATIONAL COMMAND

Similarly:

SIMULATED ACTION ≠ OPERATIONAL ACTION

The current framework does not claim authority to issue spacecraft commands, propulsion commands, attitude-control commands, navigation commands or collision-avoidance commands.

1.7 Research Philosophy

The project adopts an evidence-based research philosophy.

The development pathway is:

BUILD

↓

TEST

↓

CHALLENGE

↓

MEASURE

↓

REPEAT

↓

INDEPENDENTLY REVIEW

A visually successful demonstration is therefore only an initial research result.

Higher assurance would require progressively stronger evidence through software testing, controlled experimentation, fault injection, high-fidelity simulation, hardware-in-the-loop testing and independent verification and validation.

1.8 Independent Technical Challenge

The project welcomes independent examination of its assumptions and implementation.

Areas suitable for technical challenge include:

- architecture;
- modelling assumptions;
- scenario design;
- resilience calculations;
- state-transition logic;
- validation independence;
- recovery simulation;
- cascade behaviour;
- memory continuity;
- auditability;
- human factors;
- cybersecurity;
- software assurance;
- failure modes; and
- simulation fidelity.

The objective is not automatic acceptance.

The objective is evidence.

1.9 Research Outcome

The ultimate research outcome is intended to be measurable rather than assumed.

The key question is:

«Can a layered deterministic resilience architecture provide earlier recognition, clearer traceability and more structured decision-support when simulated orbital-system conditions begin to deteriorate?»

The Sextant Protocol™ framework provides a controlled environment in which this question can be investigated.

The answer must ultimately come from repeatable evidence and independent review.
