6. Human and Mission Authority

6.1 Purpose

The Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Framework is designed as a decision-support and research architecture.

The framework does not assume operational authority over a spacecraft, mission system, ground-control system or flight-control function.

The purpose of this layer is to investigate how structured assessment and recovery information can be presented while preserving qualified human and mission authority as the final decision authority.

The governing principle is:

HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY = FINAL

---

6.2 Decision-Support Boundary

The research framework may provide:

- scenario assessment;
- severity classification;
- resilience assessment;
- verification results;
- simulated recovery pathways;
- stability verification;
- warnings and alerts;
- escalation recommendations;
- decision-support information;
- validation results; and
- audit information.

These outputs remain within the research and simulation environment.

A recommendation generated by the framework does not constitute an operational command.

The fundamental distinction is:

DECISION-SUPPORT ≠ OPERATIONAL AUTHORITY

---

6.3 Human-in-the-Loop Principle

The architecture is intended to preserve a human decision point between simulated assessment and any simulated recovery action.

Conceptually:

SCENARIO
   ↓
ASSESSMENT
   ↓
VERIFICATION
   ↓
RECOVERY PATHWAY
   ↓
HUMAN / MISSION REVIEW
   ↓
SIMULATED DECISION
   ↓
SIMULATED RESPONSE

The purpose of this structure is to prevent an upstream analytical result from automatically becoming an operational action.

---

6.4 Mission Authority

For research purposes, the framework recognises that mission authority must remain outside the autonomous analytical layers.

The framework therefore does not claim authority to:

- command a spacecraft;
- alter a spacecraft trajectory;
- command propulsion;
- command attitude-control systems;
- command navigation systems;
- command communications systems;
- initiate collision-avoidance manoeuvres;
- override mission procedures; or
- replace qualified mission personnel.

Any displayed decision within the simulator represents a research decision state only.

---

6.5 Human Decision States

The research architecture may represent decision states such as:

- AUTHORIZE_RECOVERY
- MAINTAIN_SAFE_STATE
- REQUEST_ADDITIONAL_DIAGNOSTICS
- ABORT_RECOVERY
- ESCALATE_TO_MISSION_AUTHORITY

These states are representations for simulation and research.

They do not constitute live spacecraft commands.

The architecture is specifically intended to make the authority chain visible and auditable.

---

A representative authority chain is:

ENVIRONMENT
   ↓
ORBITAL ASSESSMENT
   ↓
SECONDARY / SUPPORT ASSESSMENT
   ↓
FAILSAFE TRANSITION
   ↓
VALIDATION
   ↓
RECOVERY SIMULATION
   ↓
HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY
   ↓

   ↓

The separation of these stages allows researchers to determine where an assessment originated, how it was verified and how the final simulated decision was reached.

---

6.7 No Automatic Execution

The current research framework does not provide an automatic operational execution pathway.

In particular:

NO RECOVERY ACTION IS EXECUTED AS A REAL SPACECRAFT ACTION.

Any recovery pathway displayed by the simulator is a simulated research result.

This distinction is maintained regardless of the severity classification produced by the simulation.

---

6.8 Human Override and Safe-State Research

Future research may investigate how human operators respond when the system produces conflicting or deteriorating assessments.

Possible research conditions include:

- incomplete telemetry;
- conflicting sensor information;
- communication degradation;
- repeated anomaly detection;
- multiple simultaneous abnormalities;
- uncertain recovery readiness;
- validation failure;
- false-positive warnings; and
- rapidly developing cascade conditions.

The purpose of such testing would be to determine whether the architecture provides useful information without creating inappropriate automation bias or unnecessary operator workload.

---

6.9 Human Factors

Human-factors evaluation is an essential component of any future higher-assurance research programme.

Potential measurements include:

- operator response time;
- comprehension of system state;
- recommendation acceptance rate;
- recommendation rejection rate;
- workload;
- alert fatigue;
- automation bias;
- situation awareness;
- decision confidence;
- recovery-pathway comprehension; and
- error recovery.

A technically deterministic system does not automatically produce good human decisions.

Human interaction must therefore be independently evaluated.

---

6.10 Captain AI Lena and Authority Separation

The wider Sextant Protocol™ architecture includes the research concept of Captain AI Lena as a supervisory intelligence layer.

The conceptual sequence is:

OBSERVE
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
ASSESS
   ↓
ARBITRATE
   ↓
RECOMMEND
   ↓
DECIDE
   ↓
ACT
   ↓
UPDATE

Within the current orbital research framework, this sequence represents a research architecture rather than autonomous spacecraft authority.

The distinction remains:

SUPERVISORY INTELLIGENCE ≠ SPACECRAFT COMMAND AUTHORITY

Any future autonomous execution capability would require a separate engineering, safety, cybersecurity, human-factors, verification and regulatory assessment programme.

---

6.11 Governance Principle

The framework adopts the following governance principle:

«No analytical module should be permitted to silently acquire operational authority merely because it produces a high-confidence result.»

Confidence is an assessment property.

Authority is a governance property.

These are separate concerns.

The research architecture therefore seeks to maintain an explicit separation between:

- assessment;
- verification;
- recommendation;
- authority; and
- execution.

---

6.12 Research Questions

This section supports investigation of questions including:

1. Does explicit human authority improve decision traceability?
2. Can structured recommendations reduce ambiguity during degraded conditions?
3. Does the authority boundary prevent inappropriate automatic execution?
4. How do operators respond to conflicting analytical assessments?
5. Does the presentation of recovery pathways influence operator decisions?
6. Can the architecture preserve human situation awareness during complex scenarios?
7. What level of automation, if any, could be justified by future evidence?

These questions remain open research questions.

---

6.13 Operational Boundary

The current framework does not connect its simulated authority layer to a real spacecraft or operational mission-control system.

There is no claimed:

- live spacecraft connection;
- live command pathway;
- propulsion interface;
- attitude-control interface;
- navigation command interface;
- autonomous flight-control interface; or
- operational mission authority.

The current implementation remains a research and simulation environment.

---

6.14 Research Integrity

Sextant Protocol™ does not claim that human-in-the-loop architecture alone guarantees safety.

The research objective is to determine, through repeatable evidence, whether structured human authority combined with deterministic assessment and validation can provide a useful resilience and decision-support layer.

Negative results remain valid research outcomes.

The framework therefore follows:

TEST → CHALLENGE → MEASURE → REPEAT → INDEPENDENTLY REVIEW

---

6.15 Summary

The Human and Mission Authority layer establishes a deliberate boundary between computational assessment and operational authority.

The research architecture may assess, verify, simulate and recommend.

It does not claim the authority to command.

The governing principle remains:

HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY = FINAL

SIMULATED ACTION ≠ OPERATIONAL ACTION

DECISION-SUPPORT ≠ OPERATIONAL COMMAND

This separation is a fundamental component of the Sextant Protocol™ research architecture.
