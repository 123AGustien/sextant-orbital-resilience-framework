SEXTANT PROTOCOL™

5. Failsafe Transition Architecture

5.1 Purpose

The Failsafe Transition Architecture is a research layer within the Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Framework.

Its purpose is to investigate how a simulated system may transition from an abnormal or degraded condition toward a controlled state while maintaining verification, traceability and human or mission authority.

The architecture does not claim to guarantee spacecraft safety or prevent physical system failure.

It provides a deterministic simulation structure for examining state transitions and recovery preparation.

---

5.2 Research Principle

The principal research principle is:

DETECT → VERIFY → STABILIZE → PREPARE → AUTHORIZE

The architecture is intended to prevent an unverified assessment from directly becoming an operational action.

---

5.3 Conceptual State Model

A simplified research state sequence is:

NORMAL

↓

ANOMALY DETECTED

↓

ASSESSMENT

↓

VERIFICATION

↓

STABILIZED

↓

RECOVERY READY

↓

HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY

The exact transition behaviour remains subject to scenario definition and validation.

---

5.4 Abnormal Condition Detection

The transition layer receives simulated information from upstream assessment components.

Potential triggers include:

- communications degradation;
- orbital-state deviation;
- telemetry inconsistency;
- power-system degradation;
- inertial-state inconsistency; and
- other defined research anomalies.

Detection does not itself constitute confirmation.

A detected condition must proceed through the defined verification pathway.

---

5.5 Verification Gate

The verification stage provides a control point between detection and subsequent state transition.

Conceptually:

DETECTED

↓

VERIFY

↓

CONFIRMED / UNCONFIRMED

A confirmed condition may proceed toward stabilization or recovery assessment.

An unconfirmed condition may remain under observation, request additional diagnostics or return to a defined safe research state.

This mechanism is intended to reduce uncontrolled propagation of uncertain assessments.

---

5.6 Stabilization

The stabilization state represents a controlled research condition in which the architecture has identified an abnormal event but has not assumed that immediate recovery execution is required.

The objective is to investigate whether maintaining a stable state can provide additional time for:

- verification;
- diagnostics;
- assessment;
- recovery-pathway evaluation; and
- human or mission decision-making.

The stabilization state is therefore a research representation rather than a certified spacecraft operating mode.

---

5.7 Recovery Preparation

Following verification and stabilization, the framework may generate a simulated recovery pathway.

A conceptual sequence is:

STABILIZED

↓

RECOVERY ASSESSMENT

↓

RECOVERY PATHWAY

↓

STABILITY VERIFICATION

↓

RECOVERY READY

The recovery pathway remains a simulation result.

No live spacecraft command is generated by this research layer.

---

5.8 Human / Mission Authority Gate

The final transition toward any simulated recovery action remains subject to human or mission authority.

The governing principle is:

NO OPERATIONAL ACTION WITHOUT AUTHORISED HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY

Within the research environment, possible authority outcomes may include:

- maintain safe state;
- request additional diagnostics;
- authorise simulated recovery;
- abort simulated recovery;
- or escalate to mission authority.

These represent research decisions only.

---

5.9 Cascade Prevention Research

The architecture is also intended to support research into cascading conditions.

A conceptual cascade may be represented as:

INITIAL ANOMALY

↓

SYSTEM STRESS

↓

SECONDARY EFFECT

↓

REDUCED SYSTEM MARGIN

↓

ADDITIONAL ANOMALY

↓

CASCADE RISK

↓

STABILIZATION / RECOVERY ASSESSMENT

Research can then examine whether earlier state recognition produces measurable differences compared with a defined baseline.

The framework does not claim that cascade prevention is guaranteed.

---

5.10 Deterministic Transition Logic

For equivalent inputs and defined system conditions, the research objective is to produce consistent transition behaviour.

This allows researchers to investigate:

- repeatability;
- transition consistency;
- unexpected state changes;
- invalid transitions;
- recovery-pathway consistency;
- and fault response.

Determinism is therefore treated as a research property to be measured and verified rather than assumed.

---

5.11 Failure and Fault Injection

Future testing may intentionally introduce:

- missing data;
- conflicting assessments;
- delayed telemetry;
- corrupted inputs;
- unavailable components;
- repeated anomalies;
- simultaneous abnormalities; and
- invalid state-transition requests.

The purpose is to determine whether the transition architecture responds predictably and whether failures remain contained within the intended research boundaries.

---

5.12 Auditability

Every significant transition should, where implemented, produce an auditable record containing sufficient information to reconstruct the simulated sequence.

A conceptual record is:

EVENT → STATE → ASSESSMENT → VERIFICATION → TRANSITION → DECISION → RESULT

This supports independent examination of the simulation rather than relying solely on the final displayed state.

---

5.13 Operational Boundary

The Failsafe Transition Architecture is not:

- a spacecraft flight-control system;
- a propulsion controller;
- an attitude-control system;
- an autonomous navigation controller;
- a collision-avoidance system;
- or a certified safety-critical control mechanism.

All transitions described in this research document are simulated representations.

---

5.14 Research Objective

The principal research question for this architecture is:

«Can deterministic state-transition and stabilization logic provide a more controlled and traceable response to developing abnormal conditions within a simulated orbital environment?»

The answer must be established through evidence, repeatable testing and independent review.

SIMULATION FIRST.

VALIDATION BEFORE ASSURANCE.

HUMAN / MISSION AUTHORITY REMAINS FINAL.
