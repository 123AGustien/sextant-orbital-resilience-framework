SEXTANT PROTOCOL™

2. Research Proposition

Central Proposition

The central research proposition of the Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Framework is:

«A layered deterministic resilience architecture combining independent assessment, controlled state transitions, validation, memory continuity, auditability and human decision authority may improve the recognition and structured management of developing abnormal conditions in a controlled orbital simulation environment.»

This proposition remains a research hypothesis.

The framework does not present the proposition as an established engineering fact, safety claim or operational capability.

Its purpose is to provide an environment in which the proposition can be examined through repeatable simulation, controlled scenarios, fault injection, measurement and independent technical review.

Research Questions

The research seeks to determine whether the architecture can:

1. identify developing abnormal conditions in a consistent manner;
2. distinguish between normal, degraded and critical simulated states;
3. provide controlled transitions between system states;
4. identify conflicting or inconsistent assessment results;
5. provide repeatable recovery-pathway simulations;
6. maintain traceability across sequential scenarios;
7. preserve a clear authority chain;
8. provide auditable evidence of how a simulated decision was produced; and
9. provide useful human-in-the-loop decision-support without assuming operational authority.

Null Position

The research does not assume that the Sextant architecture will outperform conventional approaches.

A valid research outcome may demonstrate:

- no measurable improvement;
- equivalent performance;
- increased complexity;
- unexpected failure modes;
- unacceptable false-positive or false-negative behaviour;
- human-factors limitations; or
- other deficiencies requiring redesign.

Such findings would constitute legitimate research results.

Evidence Principle

A successful demonstration of one or more simulated scenarios shall not, by itself, constitute evidence of operational safety.

Any claim of higher assurance would require progressively stronger evidence through appropriate:

- software verification;
- scenario validation;
- fault-injection testing;
- baseline comparison;
- human-factors evaluation;
- high-fidelity simulation;
- hardware-in-the-loop testing;
- independent verification and validation; and
- specialist aerospace and mission-specific review.

Fundamental Research Principle

THE FRAMEWORK DOES NOT CLAIM TO GUARANTEE RESILIENCE.

It investigates whether resilience can be measured, modelled and improved through structured architecture and repeatable evidence.

Research Integrity

Sextant Protocol™ therefore maintains the following principle:

CLAIMS MUST REMAIN PROPORTIONAL TO EVIDENCE.
