ORBITAL RESILIENCE RESEARCH WHITE PAPER

1. Research Context

Modern spacecraft and mission systems operate through increasingly complex interactions between navigation, communications, power, telemetry, guidance, software, ground operations and autonomous or semi-autonomous decision-support.

A disturbance in one subsystem can therefore produce secondary effects elsewhere in the system. The resulting interaction may create degraded system margins, conflicting assessments, delayed recognition or cascading conditions.

The Sextant Protocol™ Orbital Resilience Framework investigates whether a layered deterministic resilience architecture can provide structured recognition, verification, state management, recovery assessment and decision-support before an abnormal condition develops into a larger cascading event.

The research does not assume that such an architecture is automatically safe, superior or suitable for operational spacecraft.

Instead, the framework establishes a controlled research and simulation environment in which the proposition can be:

- tested;
- challenged;
- measured;
- repeated;
- audited; and
- independently reviewed.

Research Scope

The current research scope includes:

- orbital-system scenario simulation;
- abnormal-condition detection;
- severity and resilience assessment;
- controlled state transitions;
- recovery-pathway simulation;
- validation and consistency checking;
- memory continuity;
- audit traceability;
- cascade-resilience research;
- human-in-the-loop decision-support; and
- future fault-injection studies.

Research Boundary

The framework remains a simulation and research environment.

It does not provide certified spacecraft control, navigation, propulsion, attitude-control or collision-avoidance functionality.

The architecture deliberately separates assessment and simulation from operational command authority.

ASSESSMENT ≠ OPERATIONAL COMMAND

Research Objective

The principal objective is to determine, through repeatable evidence, whether structured resilience layers can improve recognition, traceability and decision-support during simulated orbital-system degradation.

The project therefore welcomes independent technical challenge and accepts both positive and negative research results.

Guiding Principle

THE FRAMEWORK DOES NOT SEEK AUTOMATIC ACCEPTANCE OF ITS CLAIMS.

IT SEEKS EVIDENCE.

The research pathway is:

BUILD → TEST → CHALLENGE → MEASURE → REPEAT → INDEPENDENTLY REVIEW
